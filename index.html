Researcher · Engineer · PhD Applicant

I study perovskite solar cells — from their fundamental material properties and optoelectronic behaviour, through the defects and structural choices that govern device performance, all the way to the scalability challenges that stand between a promising lab result and a technology the world can actually use. My BSc thesis investigated silver nanoparticle interlayer engineering in simulated perovskite solar cells; a published ML + SCAPS-1D study (IJER, Wiley 2026) subsequently identified CuSbS₂ as an optimal HTL for lead-free RbGeI₃ cells, yielding a 28.32% simulated PCE. My work spans first-principles DFT, device simulation, and machine learning, shaped by over three years of real-world materials engineering in industrial production.

02 — Research

Research Interests

Perovskite Material Properties

Understanding the fundamental electronic, optical, and structural properties of perovskite compounds through DFT-based first-principles calculations, SCAPS-1D device simulation, microstructural analysis, and optoelectronic characterisation — building a bottom-up picture of what makes a perovskite material photovoltaically promising.

Click to see my work

Key Factors Governing Photovoltaic Performance

Investigating how intrinsic device parameters — defect density, carrier trap states, layer thickness, interface quality, and recombination pathways — determine the photovoltaic output metrics of perovskite cells. Identifying which factors have the highest leverage on efficiency and stability is central to my research approach.

Click to see my work

Device Architecture & Parameter Optimization

Systematically optimising perovskite solar cell architectures by selecting and tuning electron transport layers (ETL), hole transport layers (HTL), and their interlayer interactions. Applying machine learning-assisted simulation to navigate the high-dimensional parameter space and maximise power conversion efficiency beyond what conventional sweeps can achieve.

Click to see my work

Scalability, Longevity & Manufacturability

Addressing the critical gap between laboratory performance and real-world deployment — studying how fabrication techniques, working temperature, environmental stability, and encapsulation strategies affect device lifetime and manufacturing viability. My industrial engineering background gives me a grounded perspective on what scalability actually demands.

Future Research Direction

My near-term research goal is to advance the long-term operational stability of perovskite solar cells — the critical bottleneck preventing commercial deployment. I aim to combine computational screening of novel perovskite compositions with experimental fabrication and characterization, ultimately contributing to devices that are both highly efficient and durable under real-world operating conditions. My industrial background in materials processing adds a manufacturing-aware perspective that I believe is essential for translating laboratory breakthroughs into scalable energy solutions.

Research Projects

Performance Evaluation of Nanoparticle-Based Interlayered Perovskite Solar Cell
BSc Thesis
Apr 2021 – Mar 2022  ·  KUET, Khulna

Numerical investigation into plasmonic enhancement of perovskite solar cell efficiency using nanoparticle-based interlayers, simulated in SETFOS (Fluxim AG) with Transfer Matrix Method optical modelling combined with drift-diffusion electrical simulation. Compared Ag and Au nanoparticles at varying sizes and positions within a TiO₂ / CH₃NH₃PbI₃ / SpiroOMeTAD device stack to identify the configuration yielding the highest photovoltaic performance.

▶ SETFOS simulations evaluated silver nanoparticle size and interlayer placement, including stepwise efficiency changes. Results are numerical simulations; no experimental fabrication was conducted.

Unveiling the Photovoltaic Potential of CsSnF₃: First-Principles + SCAPS-1D Study
Research · Unpublished Draft
2024  ·  Lead Investigator  ·  KUET, Khulna

Conducted the first device-level simulation of a CsSnF₃-based lead-free perovskite solar cell, combining DFT calculations (CASTEP, GGA-PBE functional) with SCAPS-1D device simulation. DFT analysis confirmed a direct band gap of 1.707 eV at the R-symmetry point and high optical absorption (approaching 10⁵ cm⁻¹). SCAPS-1D was then used to simulate a full ITO/WO₃/CsSnF₃/CuI/Au device stack, optimising absorber thickness, carrier concentration, defect densities, transport layer thicknesses, and back contact work function.

▶ Optimised device achieved a simulated PCE of 30.72% (Voc = 1.62 V, Jsc = 21.94 mA/cm², FF = 86.37%). CsSnF₃ accesses high efficiency through a Voc-dominant pathway, in contrast to the Jsc-dominant route of CsSnI₃. All values from numerical simulation; experimental fabrication is future work.

DFT (CASTEP/GGA-PBE) SCAPS-1D CsSnF₃ Perovskite Lead-Free PV Band Structure Device Optimisation
High-Efficiency Lead-Free FASnI₃ Perovskite Solar Cell Optimisation
Research · Manuscript Under Review
2024–2025  ·  Collaborative Study  ·  KUET / CUET

Collaborated on a systematic SCAPS-1D simulation study of a novel FTO/CdS/FASnI₃/V₂O₅/Au device architecture. Investigated the combined influence of absorber thickness and bulk defect density, ETL and HTL thickness and defect quality, and interfacial defect densities at both junctions. CdS was adopted as the ETL for its favourable conduction band alignment with FASnI₃, and V₂O₅ as the HTL for its efficient hole extraction and electron-blocking capability. Temperature stability and back contact work function effects were also characterised.

▶ Optimised device achieved a simulated PCE of 30.92% (Voc = 1.19 V, Jsc = 29.88 mA/cm², FF = 87.18%). All values from SCAPS-1D numerical simulation.

SCAPS-1D FASnI₃ Perovskite Lead-Free PV CdS ETL V₂O₅ HTL Device Optimisation

Process Optimization of 58-Ton Induction Furnace Operations
Industry
Jul 2023 – Present  ·  Bangladesh Steel Re-Rolling Mills Ltd., Chattogram  ·  Senior Production Engineer from July 2026

Led systematic process optimization of a 58-ton induction furnace producing international-grade steel. Analyzed tap-to-tap cycle data to identify bottlenecks, implemented revised charge sequencing and temperature control protocols, and optimized ferroalloy addition schedules based on melt chemistry targets.

▶ Reduced tap-to-tap cycle time from 101 to 99 minutes — a 2-minute reduction representing approximately 2% improvement in cycle throughput under constant operating conditions. Separately, reduced ferroalloy consumption from 13 kg/ton to 10.5 kg/ton (approximately 19% reduction), generating estimated annual cost savings of approximately 3–4 crores BDT.

05 — About & Background

About & Background

I am a Materials Science and Engineering graduate from Khulna University of Engineering and Technology (KUET), Bangladesh, where I ranked 16th in my cohort of 65 students. My academic journey was defined by a growing fascination with the materials physics underlying next-generation solar energy technologies — a thread that runs from my undergraduate thesis through my current research manuscripts.

Since graduating, I have worked at Bangladesh Steel Re-Rolling Mills Limited, progressing to Senior Production Engineer (effective July 2026), in Chattogram, operating and optimizing a 58-ton induction furnace in an industrial steel melting environment. While this sits outside the solar cell domain, it has given me an irreplaceable understanding of how materials behave under real process constraints — knowledge that I believe is essential for any researcher serious about translating lab-scale breakthroughs into manufacturable technologies.

In parallel with my industry role, I have continued active research: co-authoring a published study on Al-Sn-Cu alloy microstructure, leading a machine learning-assisted perovskite optimization study (now published in International Journal of Energy Research), and contributing to a first-principles optoelectronic characterization project. One further manuscript from this work is currently under review; another remains an unpublished draft not yet submitted for peer review.

I am now seeking PhD or thesis-based Masters positions in photovoltaics, computational materials science, or related fields — particularly in North America and Europe — where I can pursue the stability and scalability challenges that I believe will define the next decade of perovskite solar cell research.
